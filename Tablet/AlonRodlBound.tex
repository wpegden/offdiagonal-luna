\begin{lemma}
Let $G$ be an $(n,d,\lambda)$-graph and let $B$ be a vertex subset.  If
$A$ is any subset for which every $u\in A$ satisfies
\[
 |N_G(u)\cap B|\leq \frac{d|B|}{2n},
\]
then
\[
 |A||B|\leq \frac{4\lambda^2}{d^2}n^2.
\]
The $(n,d,\lambda)$ premise is used through the expander-mixing inequality
$|e_G(X,Y)-d|X||Y|/n|\leq\lambda\sqrt{|X||Y|}$ for all $X,Y$, and the
Lean statement takes this inequality as its explicit hypothesis.
This is the product estimate used to mark cheap extensions in the tuple
trees.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply the expander-mixing estimate of \noderef{ExpanderMixing} to $A$ and
$B$.  The hypothesis on every vertex of $A$ gives
$e_G(A,B)\leq d|A||B|/(2n)$.  Thus the main term in the mixing estimate is
at least $d|A||B|/(2n)$, and consequently
\[
 \frac d{2n}|A||B|\leq \lambda\sqrt{|A||B|}.
\]
If $|A||B|=0$ the conclusion is immediate.  Otherwise divide by its square
root and square; since $d>0$, this yields
$|A||B|\leq4\lambda^2n^2/d^2$, exactly as claimed.
\end{proof}
