\begin{lemma}
For every $s\geq4$, the specialized digraph $D_2(s)$ of
\noderef{F2PairDigraph} is loopless and $T_s$-free, and its exact number of
vertices is
\[
 |V(D_2(s))|=(2^{s-1}-1)2^{s-2}
 =2^{2s-3}-2^{s-2}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $p=s-1$ and let $V=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^p$, with inner product
$\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{r=1}^p x_r y_r$.  By the definition of
\noderef{F2PairDigraph}, the graph used to form $D_2(s)$ has vertex set
$V$, adjacency $x\mathrel{G}y$ exactly when $\langle x,y\rangle=0$, and
$D_2(s)$ is its old pair digraph.  Consequently a pair $(x,y)$ is a
vertex of $D_2(s)$ exactly when $\langle x,y\rangle\ne0$, hence exactly
when $\langle x,y\rangle=1$.

We now verify explicitly the hypothesis needed for
\noderef{OldPairDigraphProperties}.  Suppose that
$a,b:\{1,\ldots,s\}\to V$ satisfy
\[
 \langle a_i,b_i\rangle\ne0\quad(1\leq i\leq s),
 \qquad \langle a_i,b_j\rangle=0\quad(1\leq i<j\leq s).
\]
The first condition says $\langle a_i,b_i\rangle=1$, since the field has
only the two elements $0$ and $1$.  We claim that the vectors
$a_1,\ldots,a_s$ are linearly independent.  If
$\sum_{i=1}^s c_i a_i=0$ is a linear relation with some coefficient
$c_i\ne0$, choose the largest index $j$ for which $c_j\ne0$.  Pairing the
relation with $b_j$ gives
\[
 0=\sum_{i=1}^s c_i\langle a_i,b_j\rangle.
\]
For $i<j$ the summands vanish by the forward orthogonality assumption;
for $i>j$ they vanish because of the choice of $j$; and the $i=j$
summand is $c_j\langle a_j,b_j\rangle=c_j$.  Thus $c_j=0$, a
contradiction.  Hence all coefficients in a relation vanish.  But $V$
has dimension $p=s-1$, so it cannot contain $s$ linearly independent
vectors.  This contradiction proves the required triangular hypothesis.

Applying \noderef{OldPairDigraphProperties} to this verified hypothesis
gives both that $D_2(s)$ is loopless and that
$\operatorname{Nonempty}(T_s(D_2(s)))$ is false.  In the latter statement,
the ordered vertices and their forward arcs are precisely the data in the
definition of \noderef{TransitiveTournament}, so this is the asserted
$T_s$-freeness.  (Looplessness can also be seen directly: a loop at
$(x,y)$ would require $\langle x,y\rangle=0$, while vertex membership
requires $\langle x,y\rangle=1$.)

For the count, there are $2^p-1$ choices for the nonzero vector $x$.  For
each such $x$, the equation $\langle x,y\rangle=1$ defines an affine
hyperplane with exactly $2^{p-1}$ solutions: choose a coordinate $r$ with
$x_r=1$, choose the other $p-1$ coordinates of $y$ freely, and then the
equation uniquely determines $y_r$.  Every such $y$ is automatically
nonzero, since the zero vector has inner product zero with $x$.  Conversely
every vertex pair has this form, and the construction uses actual vectors,
not projective equivalence classes.  Therefore the ordered nonedge-pair
count is
\[
 (2^p-1)2^{p-1}=2^{2p-1}-2^{p-1}=2^{2s-3}-2^{s-2}.
\]
For clarity, the expression $(2^p-1)(2^{p-1}-1)$ sometimes printed for
this construction counts the different set of ordered orthogonal pairs:
for each nonzero $x$, it counts the nonzero vectors in the kernel
$\langle x,y\rangle=0$.  The vertices here are nonedge pairs, for which
$\langle x,y\rangle=1$, so the affine fiber has $2^{p-1}$ solutions and
the displayed corrected count is the one relevant to $D_2(s)$, rather than
the orthogonal-pair count.
\end{proof}
