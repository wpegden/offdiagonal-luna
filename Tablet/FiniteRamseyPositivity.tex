\begin{lemma}
For all integers $s,k\geq2$, the finite Ramsey number $r(s,k)$ is positive.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The finite Ramsey theorem gives a finite integer $R$ such that every loopless
graph on $R$ vertices has an $s$-clique or a $k$-independent set.  Thus the
set of natural numbers in the definition of \noderef{RamseyNumber} is
nonempty and contains a positive integer.  Its natural infimum is therefore
positive: if it were zero, the defining property at order zero would have to
hold, but the unique empty graph has neither an injective $s$-vertex clique
witness nor an injective $k$-vertex independent witness when $s,k\geq2$.
\end{proof}
