\begin{helper}
Let $G$ be a finite ordered graph satisfying the bilinear mixing estimate.
Let $q,t,n,d$ be natural numbers with $q\geq16$, $t\geq2$, $n>0$, and
$d>0$.
Suppose $P,Z_\ell,Z_r$ are finite vertex sets with
$|Z_r|\leq|Z_\ell|$ and
\[
 |P|\,|Z_\ell|\leq1024q^{t+1},\qquad d/n\leq4/q,
 \qquad \lambda\sqrt{|P||Z_r|}\leq128q^t.
\]
Then the number of ordered edges from $P$ to $Z_r$ is at most
$5000q^t$.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Apply the mixing inequality to $P$ and $Z_r$.  The main term is at most
\[
 (4/q)|P||Z_r|\leq(4/q)|P||Z_\ell|
 \leq4096q^t.
\]
 The error term is at most $128q^t$ by the displayed decay hypothesis.
The sum is at most $4224q^t$, hence at most $5000q^t$.  This is precisely
the estimate used after the product bound for the poor first coordinates.
\end{proof}
