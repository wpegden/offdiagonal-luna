\begin{lemma}
Let $K$ be a finite field with $q=|K|$ a prime power satisfying $q\geq16$,
and let $t\geq2$.  Put
$G=G_K(t)=\operatorname{PolarityGraph}(K,t)$ and
$D=\operatorname{OldPairDigraph}(G)$, using
\noderef{PolarityGraph} and \noderef{OldPairDigraph}.  Let
$n,d\in\mathbb N$ and $\lambda\in\mathbb R$ satisfy the parameter package
provided by \noderef{OldPolarityParameters}:
\[
 |V(G)|=n=\frac{q^{t+1}-1}{q-1},\qquad
 d=\frac{q^t-1}{q-1},
\]
$G$ is $d$-regular, its nonprincipal spectral parameter is
\[
 \lambda=\sqrt{d-\frac{q^{t-1}-1}{q-1}},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{NonprincipalSpectralBound}(G,\lambda)
 \text{ in the sense of \noderef{NonprincipalSpectralBound}},
\]
and
\[
 q^t/2\leq n\leq2q^t,\qquad
 q^{t-1}/2\leq d\leq2q^{t-1},\qquad
 \lambda\leq2\sqrt d.
\]
Assume also the expander-mixing inequality, in the ordered-edge convention,
for all vertex subsets $A,B$:
\[
 \left|e_G(A,B)-\frac d n|A||B|\right|
 \leq \lambda\sqrt{|A||B|}.
\]
Finally let $k\in\mathbb N$ satisfy $1\leq n$, $1\leq d$, and
\[
 w:=32tq\,\left\lceil\log q\right\rceil\leq k,
\]
where the ceiling is the natural-number ceiling in the Lean statement,
namely $\operatorname{Nat.ceil}(\operatorname{Real.log}q)$.  This is the
paper's natural-logarithm threshold rounded upward so that $k-w$ is an
integer exponent.
Then
\[
 \operatorname{fwi}_k(D)\leq
 8^k\left(\frac{\lambda^2}{d^2}\right)^{k-w}n^{2k}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $L=\left\lceil\log q\right\rceil$ and
$w=32tqL$.  The graph in the statement is the projective orthogonality
graph supplied by \noderef{PolarityGraph}, and its exact parameter package is
the one recorded in \noderef{OldPolarityParameters}.  The ordered nonedge
vertices and the arc rule used below are precisely those of
\noderef{OldPairDigraph}.  We use the displayed mixing inequality as an
explicit hypothesis; it is the input required by
\noderef{AlonRodlBound}, so no spectral-to-mixing implication is being used
implicitly.

First identify the objects counted by the left-hand side.  By
\noderef{OldPairDigraph}, an ordered pair $(a_i,b_i)$ is a vertex of $D$ if
and only if $a_i b_i\notin E(G)$, and an arc from $(a_i,b_i)$ to
$(a_j,b_j)$ is present if and only if $a_i b_j\in E(G)$.  Therefore, by the
forward-independence condition in \noderef{ForwardIndependentTuple}, a
forward-independent $m$-tuple in $D$ is exactly a coherent tuple
\[
 (a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_m,b_m)\in V(G)^{2m}
\]
such that $a_i b_i\notin E(G)$ for every $i$ and
$a_i b_j\notin E(G)$ whenever $i<j$.  This equivalence is reversible,
including when entries repeat, because both definitions use the same
ordered conditions and $D$ is loopless.  By the definition of
\noderef{ForwardIndependentCount}, the number of coherent tuples of length
$m$ is thus $\operatorname{fwi}_m(D)$.

Form a rooted tree $\mathcal T$ whose root is the empty tuple and whose
children are obtained by appending one coherent pair.  Its level-$k$
vertices are consequently counted by $\operatorname{fwi}_k(D)$.  Fix a
coherent partial tuple
$\sigma=(a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_m,b_m)$ and define
\[
 B^\sigma=V(G)\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^mN_G(a_i),\qquad
 A^\sigma=\left\{a\in V(G):
 |N_G(a)\cap B^\sigma|\leq\frac{d}{2n}|B^\sigma|\right\}.
\]
The definition of $A^\sigma$ gives the low-degree hypothesis for every
element of $A^\sigma$.  Applying \noderef{AlonRodlBound} to $A^\sigma$
and $B^\sigma$, with the displayed mixing inequality and $n,d>0$, gives
\[
 H:=\frac{4\lambda^2n^2}{d^2},\qquad
 |A^\sigma||B^\sigma|\leq H. \tag{1}
\]

Any coherent child appending $(a,b)$ has $b\in B^\sigma$: for every
$i\leq m$, coherence says $a_i b\notin E(G)$.  Mark precisely the children
whose appended pair lies in $A^\sigma\times B^\sigma$.  There are at most
$n^2$ children, since a child is determined by an ordered pair in
$V(G)^2$ and $|V(G)|=n$.  By (1), the number of marked children is at most
$\lfloor H\rfloor$; here $H\geq0$, so the integer floor is legitimate.  Set
$\Delta=n^2$ and $h=\lfloor H\rfloor$ for the application of
\noderef{RootedTreeCounting}.

We next prove the required bound on unmarked runs.  Put
$r=d/(2n)$.  The local hypotheses give
\[
 \frac1{4q}\leq\frac dn\leq\frac4q,
 \qquad 0\leq r\leq\frac2q\leq1. \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, the lower bound uses $d\geq q^{t-1}/2$ and $n\leq2q^t$, while
the upper bound uses $d\leq2q^{t-1}$ and $n\geq q^t/2$; positivity of
$n,d$ gives the nonnegativity in (2), and $q\geq16$ gives $2/q\leq1$.

If a child appends $(a,b)$, its next forbidden set satisfies the exact
identity
\[
 B^{\sigma\smallfrown(a,b)}=B^\sigma\setminus N_G(a),
\]
so it is always a subset of $B^\sigma$.  If the child is unmarked, then
$b\in B^\sigma$ as observed above and $(a,b)\notin A^\sigma\times
B^\sigma$ forces $a\notin A^\sigma$.  The definition of $A^\sigma$ then
gives the strict inequality
\[
 |N_G(a)\cap B^\sigma|>r|B^\sigma|,
 \qquad
 |B^{\sigma\smallfrown(a,b)}|
 =|B^\sigma|-|N_G(a)\cap B^\sigma|
 <(1-r)|B^\sigma|. \tag{3}
\]
The first set-inclusion observation shows that marked extensions never
increase the cardinality.  Induction on the number $u$ of unmarked
extensions along any root-to-vertex path now gives
\[
 |B^\sigma|\leq n(1-r)^u. \tag{4}
\]
For the induction, before the first unmarked extension use
$|B^\sigma|\leq|V(G)|=n$.  A marked extension preserves the induction
inequality by inclusion, while (3) multiplies the previous bound by
$1-r\geq0$ and makes it strict at an unmarked extension.  This proves (4)
with all intervening marked extensions included.

Suppose a root-to-vertex path had more than $w$ unmarked vertices.  It then
has at least $w+1$ unmarked extensions, so (4), monotonicity of powers in
$[0,1]$, and $w+1\geq w-1$ yield
\[
 |B^\sigma|\leq n(1-r)^{w-1}. \tag{5}
\]
The ceiling definition gives
\[
\log q\leq L<\log q+1.
\]
Since $q\geq16$, we have $L\geq1$.  Also $w=32tqL\geq2$,
so $w-1\geq w/2=16tqL$.  Using $1-x\leq\exp(-x)$ for $0\leq x\leq1$,
the bounds in (2), and the size estimate $n\leq2q^t$, (5) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |B^\sigma|
 &\leq 2q^t\exp\left(-r\,16tqL\right)\\
 &\leq 2q^t\exp(-2tL). \tag{6}
 \end{aligned}
\]
For completeness, the last expression is strictly smaller than $1$ with
the ceiling of the natural logarithm used here.  Since $\log q\leq L$,
monotonicity of the exponential gives
\[
2q^t\exp(-2tL)\leq2\exp(-tL).
\]
Since $q\geq16$ implies $L\geq\log16>2$ and $t\geq2$, we have
$tL>\log2$, so $2\exp(-tL)<1$.  Thus (6) says $|B^\sigma|<1$, forcing
$B^\sigma=\varnothing$.  But coherence puts the final second coordinate
$b_m$ in $B^\sigma$: it is nonadjacent to $a_m$ by vertex membership and
to every earlier $a_i$ by the cross conditions.  This contradiction proves
that every root-to-vertex path has at most $w$ unmarked extensions.

It remains to check the hypotheses of \noderef{RootedTreeCounting}.  From
the exact formula $d=(q^t-1)/(q-1)=1+q+\cdots+q^{t-1}$ and $t\geq2$,
$d\geq q\geq16$.  The exact square-root formula for $\lambda$ gives
$\lambda\geq0$, and the displayed bound $\lambda\leq2\sqrt d$ gives
$\lambda^2\leq4d$.  Consequently
\[
 0\leq H=\frac{4\lambda^2n^2}{d^2}\leq\frac{16n^2}{d}\leq n^2=\Delta,
\]
so $h=\lfloor H\rfloor\leq\Delta$.  Since $k\geq w$, the cited
rooted-tree estimate, with true entries recording unmarked extensions,
gives
\[
 \operatorname{fwi}_k(D)
 \leq 2^k(n^2)^w\lfloor H\rfloor^{k-w}
 \leq 2^k(n^2)^w H^{k-w}.
\]
Substituting the definition of $H$ and collecting powers yields
\[
 \operatorname{fwi}_k(D)
 \leq 2^k4^{k-w}
 \left(\frac{\lambda^2}{d^2}\right)^{k-w}n^{2k}
 \leq 8^k
 \left(\frac{\lambda^2}{d^2}\right)^{k-w}n^{2k},
\]
because $0\leq w\leq k$ and $2^k4^{k-w}\leq2^k4^k=8^k$.
\end{proof}
