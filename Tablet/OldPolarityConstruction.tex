\begin{lemma}
For every $\delta>0$ there is an integer $L>0$ such that whenever
$s\geq L$ and $k\geq Ls$, one can choose a power of two $q$ satisfying
\[
 q\leq\frac{\delta}{200}\frac{k/s}{\log(k/s)}\leq2q.
\]
The choice can be made with $q\geq16$, so $q$ is a prime power.  One can
then choose a finite field $K$ with $|K|=q$ and put $t=s-2$, with
$t\geq2$.  Let
$G=G_K(t)=\operatorname{PolarityGraph}(K,t)$ be the polarity graph of
\noderef{PolarityGraph}, and let $D=\mathcal D(G)$ be the old pair digraph
of \noderef{OldPairDigraph}.  The graph $G$ has no two-row triangular
configuration of length $s$: there are no pairs $(a_i,b_i)_{i=1}^s$ with
$a_i b_i\notin E(G)$ and $a_i b_j\in E(G)$ for every $i<j$.  Moreover,
$D$ is $T_s$-free,
\[
 |V(D)|\geq\frac{q^{2(s-2)}}2,
\]
and
\[
 \operatorname{fwi}_k(D)\leq
 \left(32q^{\,2(s-2)-(s-3)(1-\delta/5)}\right)^k.
\]
The statement records the polarity graph's triangular obstruction as part
of the choice of $G$; this is the hypothesis used by
\noderef{OldPairDigraphProperties}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose the least power of two in the indicated interval.  For $L$ large
the interval contains such a power and $q\geq16$.  A power of two is a
prime power, and the finite-field existence theorem supplies a finite field
$K$ with exactly $q$ elements.  Put $t=s-2$; increasing $L$ if necessary
ensures $t\geq2$.  Let $G=G_K(t)$ be the projective polarity graph and put
$D=\mathcal D(G)$.

Apply \noderef{OldPolarityParameters} with this $K$, its field and finite
type structures, $h_K=|K|=q$, and the hypotheses $q\geq16$ and $t\geq2$.
It supplies the triangular obstruction for $G$ at length $t+2=s$ and the
ordered nonedge count for the actual graph $G$.  The obstruction and
\noderef{OldPairDigraphProperties} give looplessness and the absence of
$T_{t+2}=T_s$ in the actual old pair digraph $D$.  The same parameter
package gives $|V(D)|\geq q^{2t}/2$.

It remains to count forward-independent tuples in this same $D$.  By the
definition in \noderef{OldPairDigraph}, such a tuple is exactly a coherent tuple
\[
 (a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_k,b_k),\qquad
 a_i b_i\notin E(G),\quad a_i b_j\notin E(G)\ (i<j).
\]
The coherent tuples form the rooted tree obtained by appending one pair.
For a partial tuple $\sigma$, let
$B^\sigma=V(G)\setminus\bigcup_iN_G(a_i)$ and let
$A^\sigma$ consist of vertices having at most
$d|B^\sigma|/(2n)$ neighbours in $B^\sigma$.  The Alon--R\"odl estimate
\noderef{AlonRodlBound} gives
\[
 |A^\sigma||B^\sigma|\leq4\lambda^2n^2/d^2.
\]
Mark the extensions from $A^\sigma\times B^\sigma$.  Each tree vertex has
at most $\Delta=n^2$ children, and the displayed product bounds the marked
children by $h=4\lambda^2n^2/d^2$.  Every unmarked extension shrinks
$|B^\sigma|$ by at least the factor $1-d/(2n)$.  Since
$w=32tq\log q$, the polarity estimates $n\leq2q^t$ and
$d\geq q^{t-1}/2$ show that a root path has at most $w$ unmarked steps.

The coherent-tree estimate \noderef{OldCoherentTreeCount}, applied to the
parameter package for this $K$ and $G$ and to the displayed mixing
inequality, therefore yields
\[
 \operatorname{fwi}_k(D)\leq
 8^k(\lambda^2/d^2)^{k-w}n^{2k}.
\]
Finally $\lambda\leq2\sqrt d$, the selected value of $q$, and
$w\leq\delta k/5$ imply, after increasing $L$ to absorb fixed factors,
\[
 8^k(\lambda^2/d^2)^{k-w}n^{2k}
 \leq\left(32q^{\,2t-(t-1)(1-\delta/5)}\right)^k.
\]
Substituting $t=s-2$ proves the lemma.
\end{proof}
