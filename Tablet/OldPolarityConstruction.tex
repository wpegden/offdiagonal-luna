\begin{lemma}
For every $\delta>0$ there is an integer $L>0$ such that whenever
$s\geq L$ and $k\geq Ls$, one can choose a power of two $q$ satisfying
\[
 q\leq\frac{\delta}{200}\frac{k/s}{\log(k/s)}\leq2q.
\]
The choice can be made with $q\geq16$, so $q$ is a prime power.  One can
then choose a finite field $K$ with $|K|=q$ and put $t=s-2$, with
$t\geq2$.  Let
$G=G_K(t)=\operatorname{PolarityGraph}(K,t)$ be the polarity graph of
\noderef{PolarityGraph}, and let $D=\mathcal D(G)$ be the old pair digraph
of \noderef{OldPairDigraph}.  The graph $G$ has no two-row triangular
configuration of length $s$: there are no pairs $(a_i,b_i)_{i=1}^s$ with
$a_i b_i\notin E(G)$ and $a_i b_j\in E(G)$ for every $i<j$.  Moreover,
$D$ is $T_s$-free,
\[
 |V(D)|\geq\frac{q^{2(s-2)}}2,
\]
and
\[
 \operatorname{fwi}_k(D)\leq
 \left(32q^{\,2(s-2)-(s-3)(1-\delta/5)}\right)^k.
\]
The statement records the polarity graph's triangular obstruction as part
of the choice of $G$; this is the hypothesis used by
\noderef{OldPairDigraphProperties}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $x=k/s$.  Choose $L$ sufficiently large, depending only on $\delta$,
so that for every $x\geq L$ the following hold: $\log x>4$,
$(\delta/200)/\log x<1$, the interval
\[
[B/2,B],\qquad B:=\frac{\delta}{200}\frac{x}{\log x},
\]
contains a power of two, and $B\geq32$.  These are possible because
$x/\log x\to\infty$.  The same choice of $L$ ensures the final fixed
constant inequalities below.  Since $s\geq L>0$ and $k\geq Ls$, we have
$x\geq L$ and all these conditions apply.

Choose a power of two $q$ in $[B/2,B]$.  Then
$q\leq B\leq2q$ and $q\geq16$.  A power of two is a prime power, and the
finite-field existence theorem supplies a field $K$ with exactly $q$
elements.  Set $t=s-2$; increasing $L$ if necessary gives $t\geq2$.
Let
\[
 G=\operatorname{PolarityGraph}(K,t),\qquad
 D=\operatorname{OldPairDigraph}(G).
\]

Apply \noderef{OldPolarityParameters} to $K$, its field and finite type
structures, $t$, $q$, the power-of-two prime-power witness, and the
hypotheses $q\geq16$ and $t\geq2$.  It supplies the exact order and degree
parameters $n,d$, the parameter $\lambda$, the bounds
\[
 q^t/2\leq n\leq2q^t,\qquad
 q^{t-1}/2\leq d\leq2q^{t-1},
\]
the triangular obstruction for $G$ at length $t+2=s$, and the lower bound
\[
 |V(D)|\geq q^{2t}/2. \tag{1}
\]
The parameter package also supplies the bilinear spectral inequality, in
the exact form required by \noderef{ExpanderMixing}.  Apply
\noderef{ExpanderMixing} with adjacency relation $G.adj$, order $n$, degree
$d$, parameter $\lambda$, the supplied cardinality equality, and that
bilinear inequality.  This yields for every pair of vertex subsets $A,B$
the ordered estimate
\[
 \left|e_G(A,B)-\frac dn|A||B|\right|
 \leq\lambda\sqrt{|A||B|}. \tag{2}
\]
Thus the all-subsets mixing hypothesis required by
\noderef{AlonRodlBound} and \noderef{OldCoherentTreeCount} has been
derived explicitly, rather than being imported from a proof of either
node.

The triangular obstruction from \noderef{OldPolarityParameters}, together
with the actual definition of $D$ in \noderef{OldPairDigraph}, is exactly
the hypothesis of \noderef{OldPairDigraphProperties} at $s=t+2$.  Hence $D$
is loopless and contains no $T_{t+2}=T_s$, and (1) is the required vertex
lower bound.

It remains to prove the forward-independent estimate.  By
\noderef{OldPairDigraph}, a vertex of $D$ is an ordered nonedge $(a,b)$ of
$G$, and $(a,b)\to(a',b')$ precisely when $ab'\in E(G)$.  Therefore the
definition of forward independence in \noderef{ForwardIndependentTuple}
identifies a forward-independent $m$-tuple in $D$ with a coherent tuple
\[
 (a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_m,b_m)\in V(G)^{2m}
\]
satisfying $a_i b_i\notin E(G)$ for every $i$ and
$a_i b_j\notin E(G)$ for every $i<j$.  This equivalence is reversible and
allows repeated entries, because both sides use the same ordered
conditions.  By the cardinality definition in
\noderef{ForwardIndependentCount}, the level-$m$ coherent tuples are
counted by $\operatorname{fwi}_m(D)$.

We now split according to $\delta$.  First suppose that
$0<\delta\leq5$.  Put
\[
 L_q=\operatorname{Nat.ceil}(\operatorname{Real.log}q),
 \qquad w=32tqL_q.
\]
This is exactly the natural-logarithm threshold, rounded upward to an
integer, in \noderef{OldCoherentTreeCount}.  Since $q\leq x$ and $\log x>4$,
the ceiling satisfies
\[
 L_q\leq\log q+1\leq\log x+1\leq\frac54\log x.
\]
Moreover $q\geq16$ implies $L_q\geq1$, so $w=32tqL_q\geq2$.
Using the defining upper bound on $q$ and $x=k/s$, we obtain, line by line,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 w&=32tqL_q\\
  &\leq32t\left(\frac{\delta}{200}\frac{x}{\log x}\right)
       \frac54\log x\\
  &=\frac{\delta}{5}\,t\frac{k}{s}
   \leq\frac{\delta}{5}k
   \leq k.
\end{aligned} \tag{6}
\]
The penultimate inequality uses $t=s-2\leq s$, and the last uses
$\delta\leq5$.  Thus the required hypothesis $w\leq k$ is established in
this branch before the old counting lemma is used; no such conclusion is
being asserted for the other branch.

For completeness, we now give the marked-tree verification used in this
small-$\delta$ branch.  Form a rooted tree whose root is the empty tuple and
whose children append one coherent pair.  For a coherent partial tuple
$\sigma=(a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_m,b_m)$ put
\[
 B^\sigma=V(G)\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^mN_G(a_i),\qquad
 A^\sigma=\left\{a:|N_G(a)\cap B^\sigma|
       \leq\frac{d}{2n}|B^\sigma|\right\}.
\]
The already derived estimate (2) supplies the explicit mixing premise of
\noderef{AlonRodlBound}; applying that node to $A^\sigma,B^\sigma$ gives
\[
 |A^\sigma||B^\sigma|\leq H,\qquad
 H:=\frac{4\lambda^2n^2}{d^2}. \tag{3}
\]
Mark exactly the children whose appended pair belongs to
$A^\sigma\times B^\sigma$.  There are at most $n^2$ children and, by (3),
at most $\lfloor H\rfloor$ marked children.

If $(a,b)$ is any coherent extension, then $b\in B^\sigma$, because all
the conditions $a_i b\notin E(G)$ hold.  Consequently an unmarked
extension has $a\notin A^\sigma$, and hence removes strictly more than
$d|B^\sigma|/(2n)$ vertices from the next forbidden set.  More precisely,
\[
 B^{\sigma\mathbin{\smallfrown}(a,b)}=B^\sigma\setminus N_G(a),
 \qquad
 |B^{\sigma\mathbin{\smallfrown}(a,b)}|
 <\left(1-\frac d{2n}\right)|B^\sigma|. \tag{4}
\]
The first identity also shows that marked extensions never increase the
forbidden-set cardinality.  Induction along a path therefore gives
\[
 |B^\sigma|\leq n\left(1-\frac d{2n}\right)^u, \tag{5}
\]
where $u$ is the number of unmarked extensions on the path.

The parameter bounds give
\[
 \frac1{4q}\leq\frac dn\leq\frac4q,
 \qquad 0\leq\frac d{2n}\leq\frac2q\leq1. \tag{7}
\]
If a path had more than $w$ unmarked vertices, (5), (7), and
$w-1\geq w/2=16tqL_q$ would give
\[
 |B^\sigma|
 \leq 2q^t\exp(-2tL_q). \tag{8}
\]
Here we used $1-y\leq\exp(-y)$ and the weaker exponent obtained from
$d/(2n)\geq1/(8q)$; retaining the sharper bound only improves (8).
Since $\log q\leq L_q$, we have $q^t\leq\exp(tL_q)$, so the right side of
(8) is at most $2\exp(-tL_q)<1$ for $q\geq16$ and $t\geq2$.  Thus
$B^\sigma$ would be empty, contradicting that the final second coordinate
$b_m$ lies in it.  Every root-to-level-$k$ path consequently has at most
$w$ unmarked extensions.

The parameter package in \noderef{OldPolarityParameters} gives
$1\leq n$ and $1\leq d$: the displayed positive lower bounds, together
with $q\geq16$ and $t\geq2$, make these cardinality and degree parameters
positive.  Together with the mixing estimate (2), the exact definitions of
$G$ and $D$, and (6), these are precisely the hypotheses of
\noderef{OldCoherentTreeCount}.  Applying it gives
\[
 \operatorname{fwi}_k(D)\leq
 8^k\left(\frac{\lambda^2}{d^2}\right)^{k-w}n^{2k}. \tag{9}
\]

Write $a=(q^{t-1}-1)/(q-1)$.  The exact formulas in
\noderef{OldPolarityParameters} give $\lambda^2=d-a\leq d$ and
$d\geq q^{t-1}$.  Also, since $q\geq16$,
\[
 n=1+q+\cdots+q^t\leq\frac{16}{15}q^t. \tag{10}
\]
Since $0\leq w\leq k$, the first inequality below follows from
$\lambda^2/d^2\leq1/d$; the next two use (6), (9), (10), and positivity:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{fwi}_k(D)
 &\leq 8^k d^{-(k-w)}n^{2k}\\
 &\leq \left(8\left(\frac{16}{15}\right)^2\right)^k
       q^{\,2tk-(t-1)(k-w)}\\
 &\leq 32^k q^{\,2tk-(t-1)(1-\delta/5)k}\\
 &=\left(32q^{\,2t-(t-1)(1-\delta/5)}\right)^k.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $8(16/15)^2<32$, and $w\leq(\delta/5)k$ with $t-1\geq0$
justifies the exponent comparison.
Since $t=s-2$ and $t-1=s-3$, this is the asserted bound in the theorem.

Finally suppose that $\delta\geq5$.  We do not invoke
\noderef{OldCoherentTreeCount} in this case and do not use $k-w$.  Let
\[
 M=|V(D)|=\operatorname{card}(D.\mathrm{vertex}).
\]
By the ordered nonedge-pair definition in \noderef{OldPairDigraph},
$V(D)$ is a subset of $V(G)\times V(G)$.  The finiteness and cardinality
identities supplied by \noderef{OldPolarityParameters} therefore give
\[
 M\leq n^2\leq(2q^t)^2=4q^{2t}. \tag{11}
\]
The statement of \noderef{ForwardIndependentTuple} makes a
forward-independent $k$-tuple a tuple in $V(D)^k$, and
\noderef{ForwardIndependentCount} defines $\operatorname{fwi}_k(D)$ as the
cardinality of the subtype of such tuples.  Hence, after coercing
cardinalities to reals, it is at most the cardinality of the ambient tuple
type:
\[
 \operatorname{fwi}_k(D)\leq M^k\leq(4q^{2t})^k. \tag{12}
\]
Now $\delta\geq5$ implies $1-\delta/5\leq0$, while $t-1\geq0$, and so
\[
 E:=2t-(t-1)(1-\delta/5)\geq2t.
\]
Because $q\geq16>1$, monotonicity in the exponent gives
$q^{2t}\leq q^E$.  Since $4\leq32$ and all factors are nonnegative,
\[
 (4q^{2t})^k\leq(32q^E)^k. \tag{13}
\]
Combining (12) and (13), and substituting $t=s-2$ (so $t-1=s-3$), gives
the asserted bound
\[
 \operatorname{fwi}_k(D)\leq
 \left(32q^{\,2(s-2)-(s-3)(1-\delta/5)}\right)^k.
\]
This completes both cases.
\end{proof}
