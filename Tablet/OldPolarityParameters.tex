\begin{lemma}
Let $K$ be a finite field, let $q=|K|$ be a prime power with $q\geq16$,
and let $t\geq2$.  The polarity graph $G_K(t)$ of
\noderef{PolarityGraph} has order
\[
 n=\frac{q^{t+1}-1}{q-1}
\]
and is $d$-regular with
\[
 d=\frac{q^t-1}{q-1}.
\]
Write $G=G_K(t)$ below.
If $a=(q^{t-1}-1)/(q-1)$ is the common-neighbour parameter for distinct
points, its nonprincipal spectral parameter is
$\lambda=\sqrt{d-a}$, in the sense of
\noderef{NonprincipalSpectralBound}.  In particular,
\[
 q^t/2\leq n\leq2q^t,\qquad
 q^{t-1}/2\leq d\leq2q^{t-1},\qquad
 \lambda\leq2\sqrt d.
\]
The orthogonality relation has no two-row triangular configuration of length
$t+2$: there are no $(a_i,b_i)_{i=1}^{t+2}$ with
$a_i b_i\notin E(G)$ and $a_i b_j\in E(G)$ for all $i<j$.  Moreover, the
old pair digraph $\mathcal D(G)$ has at least $q^{2t}/2$ vertices.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix representatives for the points using the projectivization in
\noderef{PolarityGraph}.  There are $q^{t+1}-1$ nonzero vectors and every
one-dimensional subspace has exactly $q-1$ nonzero representatives.  Hence
the order is exactly $n=(q^{t+1}-1)/(q-1)$.  Expanding this quotient as the
geometric sum $1+q+\cdots+q^t$ gives $q^t\leq n$ and
$n\leq q^t/(1-1/q)\leq2q^t$, since $q\geq16$.  For a fixed nonzero vector
$x$,
the equation $\langle x,y\rangle=0$ is a nontrivial linear equation, so its
solution space has $q^t$ elements and has $q^t-1$ nonzero elements.  Dividing
by the $q-1$ representatives of each point shows that every vertex has
exactly $d=(q^t-1)/(q-1)$ neighbours, with a loop counted once when present.
The same geometric-sum expansion gives $q^{t-1}\leq d\leq2q^{t-1}$, so the
displayed order and degree inequalities follow.

For distinct points, independent representatives impose two independent
linear equations on $y$.  Their common orthogonal space therefore has
$q^{t-1}-1$ nonzero vectors, so the common-neighbour parameter is
$a=(q^{t-1}-1)/(q-1)$.  Thus the adjacency matrix satisfies
$A^2=aJ_n+(d-a)I_n$.  If $v$ and $\mu$ satisfy the eigen-equations in
\noderef{NonprincipalSpectralBound}, then the coordinate-sum condition gives
$J_nv=0$, and applying $A$ once more gives $\mu^2v=(d-a)v$.  Since $v\ne0$,
$\mu^2=d-a$, so $|\mu|=\sqrt{d-a}=\lambda$.  Also $0\leq a\leq d$, hence
$\lambda\leq\sqrt d\leq2\sqrt d$.

For the triangular assertion, choose nonzero representatives $x_i,y_i$.
If a nontrivial relation among the $y_i$ has first nonzero coefficient at
$i$, taking its inner product with $x_i$ leaves only the nonzero diagonal
term $\langle x_i,y_i\rangle$, a contradiction.  Thus $t+2$ vectors would
be independent in the $(t+1)$-dimensional ambient space, a contradiction.
Finally, by the ordered nonedge-pair definition in
\noderef{OldPairDigraph}, the old pair digraph has one vertex for each
ordered nonedge.  Counting the complement of the $d$ neighbours in each
first-coordinate fibre, and using the exact values of $n$ and $d$ above, gives
exactly $n(n-d)=nq^t$ ordered nonedges, because $n-d=q^t$.  Since
$n\geq q^t$, this is at least $q^{2t}$, and therefore at least
$q^{2t}/2$ as required.
\end{proof}
