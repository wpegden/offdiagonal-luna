\begin{lemma}
Let $G$ be a loopless graph with finite vertex type $G.vertex$, let
$n=|G.vertex|=\texttt{@Fintype.card G.vertex G.fintype}$, and let $s,k\in\mathbb N$.
Assume that $G$ is $K_s$-free, that $1\leq k$, and let $p\in\mathbb R$
with $0\leq p$ and $p\leq1$.  If
$p^k\,i_k(G)\leq1$ (where $i_k(G)$ is regarded as a real number), then
\[
 r(s,k)>pn-1.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $s=0$, the map from the empty indexing set to $V(G)$ is injective and
has vacuously adjacent distinct images, so \noderef{CliqueWitness} gives a
clique witness of order $0$, contrary to the $K_s$-free hypothesis.  Thus we
may assume $s\geq1$; together with $1\leq k$, this puts both Ramsey
parameters in the positive range.

Let $U$ be obtained by retaining each vertex independently with probability
$p$, and put $G'=G[U]$.  Because $G'$ is induced, the loopless condition is
inherited from $G$.  Also, a clique witness in $G'$ would, by the injectivity
and distinct-vertex adjacency in \noderef{CliqueWitness}, be a clique witness
in $G$; hence the hypothesis that $G$ is $K_s$-free implies that $G'$ is
$K_s$-free.

For the expectation, write $n=|V(G)|$ and use indicators.  Each vertex
$v$ contributes $1$ to $|V(G')|$ exactly when it is retained, so its expected
contribution is $p$, and summing over the $n$ vertices gives
$\mathbb E|V(G')|=pn$.  By the definition of $i_k$ in
\noderef{IndependentSetCount}, the independent $k$-sets of $G$ form a finite
list in which each set occurs once.  A listed set $S$ is an independent
$k$-set of $G'$ exactly when every vertex of $S$ is retained: inducedness
preserves all the required nonadjacencies, and no other vertices can enter
$S$.  Since $|S|=k$ and the vertex-retention events are independent, the
indicator of this event has expectation $p^k$.  Linearity of expectation
therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E\bigl(|V(G')|-i_k(G')\bigr)=pn-p^k i_k(G)\geq pn-1,
\]
where the final inequality is exactly the assumed count bound.  Since the
sample space is finite, an average cannot exceed every one of its values.
Thus there is an outcome, denoted by $F$, for which
\[
 |V(F)|-i_k(F)\geq pn-1.
\]

We now remove the independent $k$-sets of $F$ carefully.  By the finite-set
definition in \noderef{IndependentSetCount}, enumerate the finite list counted
by $i_k(F)$, and process its members in that order.  At a
given stage, skip a listed set if one of its vertices has already been
deleted.  Otherwise all of its $k$ vertices are still present.  The
hypothesis $1\leq k$ implies that a set of cardinality $k$ is nonempty, so
we may choose one of its vertices and delete it.  This deletes the set being
processed, and at most one vertex is deleted for each member of the list;
hence the total number of deleted vertices is at most $i_k(F)$.  Deleting
vertices cannot create a clique: any clique witness in the remaining induced
graph would also be a clique witness in $F$, so the remaining graph is still
$K_s$-free by \noderef{CliqueWitness}.  It is also loopless because looplessness
is inherited by induced subgraphs.

Call the final graph $H$ and let $m=|V(H)|$.  Every independent $k$-set of
$H$ in the finite-set sense of \noderef{IndependentSetCount} is an independent
$k$-set of $F$, because $H$ is induced.  It was
therefore one of the listed sets.  If it had not been destroyed, then it
would still have all of its vertices at the stage when it was processed, and
the procedure would have deleted one of them.  This contradiction proves
that $H$ has no independent set of size $k$.  Moreover,
\[
 m\geq |V(F)|-i_k(F)\geq pn-1.
\]

For completeness, spell out the Ramsey-number step.  Let
\[
\mathcal R_{s,k}=\{N\in\mathbb N:\text{ every loopless finite graph of
 order }N\text{ has a clique witness of order }s\text{ or an independent
 witness of order }k\}.
\]
This is the set whose natural infimum defines $r(s,k)$ in
\noderef{RamseyNumber}.  The finite Ramsey theorem shows that
$\mathcal R_{s,k}$ is nonempty, since $s,k\geq1$.  By well-ordering of
$\mathbb N$, this nonempty set has a least element $R$, and its natural
infimum is attained at that element: $r(s,k)=R$ and
$R\in\mathcal R_{s,k}$.

Suppose for contradiction that $m\geq r(s,k)=R$.  Choose $R$ vertices of
$H$ and let $H_0$ be the induced graph on them.  Then $H_0$ is loopless,
has order $R$, and is still $K_s$-free, because any clique witness in $H_0$
would be one in $H$.  It also has no independent set of size $k$, because
any such set in $H_0$ would be one in $H$.  Thus $H_0$ is a loopless graph of
order $R$ with neither of the two witnesses required by the defining
property of $\mathcal R_{s,k}$, contradicting $R\in\mathcal R_{s,k}$.
Consequently $m<r(s,k)$.  Combining this strict inequality with
$m\geq pn-1$ yields
\[
 r(s,k)>pn-1,
\]
as claimed.
\end{proof}
