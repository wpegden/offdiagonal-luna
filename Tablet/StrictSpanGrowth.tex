\begin{helper}
Let $W$ be a subspace of a finite-dimensional vector space over a field and
let $v\notin W$.  Adjoining $v$ increases the span dimension by exactly one:
\[
 \dim\bigl(W+\langle v\rangle\bigr)=\dim W+1.
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
The line $\langle v\rangle$ has dimension one because $v\ne0$, which follows
from $v\notin W$ and $0\in W$.  The intersection
$W\cap\langle v\rangle$ is zero: a nonzero member of the line is a scalar
multiple of $v$, and an inverse scalar would then put $v$ in $W$.  The
dimension formula for a sum of finite-dimensional subspaces therefore gives
\[
 \dim(W+\langle v\rangle)=\dim W+1-\dim(W\cap\langle v\rangle)
 =\dim W+1.
\]
\end{proof}
