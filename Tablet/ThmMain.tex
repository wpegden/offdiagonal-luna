\begin{theorem}
For any $s\geq3$, there is a positive constant $c_s$ such that for every
$k\geq2$,
\[
 r(s,k)\geq c_s\frac{k^{s-1}}{(\log k)^{2s-4}}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix $s\geq3$.  We first treat sufficiently large $k$.  Put $t=s-1$, and
let $C=C(t)$ be supplied by \noderef{DStarCounting}.  Choose a power of two
$q$ with
\[
 Cq(\log q)^2\leq k\leq4Cq(\log q)^2.
\]
For large $k$ such a power exists, and $q\asymp_s k/(\log k)^2$.
The cited lemma supplies a loopless $T_s$-free digraph $D$ with
$|V(D)|\geq q^{2t-1}/4$ and
$\operatorname{fwi}_k(D)\leq(Cq^t)^k$.

Apply \noderef{RandomPermutationReduction} to obtain a loopless $K_s$-free
graph $G$ on the same vertex set.  Thus
\[
 i_k(G)\leq(e/k)^k(Cq^t)^k.
\]
Set $p=k/(eCq^t)$.  The relation between $k$ and $q$, with $t\geq2$, makes
$0<p\leq1$ for large $k$, and the last display gives $p^ki_k(G)\leq1$.
The sampling lemma \noderef{SamplingDeletion} now yields
\[
 r(s,k)>p|V(G)|-1\geq\frac{kq^{t-1}}{4eC}-1.
\]
Since $q\geq c'_s k/(\log k)^2$, reducing a positive constant depending only
on $s$ gives the required bound for all sufficiently large $k$.

There are only finitely many integers $k\geq2$ below this threshold.  By
\noderef{FiniteRamseyPositivity}, all corresponding $r(s,k)$ are positive.
Choose $c_s$ smaller than the finitely many ratios
$r(s,k)(\log k)^{2s-4}/k^{s-1}$ and than the large-$k$ constant.  This
simultaneously proves the displayed inequality for every $k\geq2$.
\end{proof}
