\begin{theorem}
For every $\delta>0$, there is a constant $L$ such that for all positive
integers $s\geq L$ and $k\geq Ls$,
\[
 r(s,k)\geq\left(\frac{k}{s}\right)^{(1-\delta)s}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix $\delta>0$ and put
\[
 \delta_0=\min\{\delta,1/20\}.
\]
Then $0<\delta_0\leq\delta$ and $\delta_0\leq1/20$.  Apply
\noderef{OldPolarityConstruction} with $\delta_0$.  Increasing the resulting
threshold if necessary (which preserves the conclusion of that lemma), take
$s\geq L$, $k\geq Ls$.  It supplies a power of two $q$
with
\[
 q\leq\frac{\delta_0}{200}\frac{k/s}{\log(k/s)}\leq2q,
\]
and a loopless $T_s$-free digraph $D$ satisfying
\[
 |V(D)|\geq q^{2(s-2)}/2,\qquad
 \operatorname{fwi}_k(D)\leq
 \left(32q^{2(s-2)-(s-3)(1-\delta_0/5)}\right)^k.
\]

The random-permutation reduction \noderef{RandomPermutationReduction} gives a
loopless $K_s$-free graph $G$ on $|V(D)|$ vertices and
\[
 i_k(G)\leq
 \left(\frac{32e}{k}
 q^{2(s-2)-(s-3)(1-\delta_0/5)}\right)^k.
\]
The construction also supplies $s-2\geq2$, so $s-3\geq1$.
For $L$ sufficiently large, set
$p=q^{(s-3)(1-\delta_0/5)-2(s-2)}k/(32e)$.
The lower and upper bounds on $q$, together with $k/s\geq L$ and
$s\geq L$, allow $L=L(\delta)$ to be chosen so that $0<p\leq1$ and
$p^ki_k(G)\leq1$: indeed the latter follows by multiplying the displayed
bound for $i_k(G)$ by $p^k$, and the former is the elementary inequality
$k\leq32e\,q^{2(s-2)-(s-3)(1-\delta_0/5)}$ after increasing $L$.
Also $L>0$ gives $1\leq s$ and hence $1\leq k$, so all hypotheses of
\noderef{SamplingDeletion} are now explicit.  By that lemma,
\[
 r(s,k)>p|V(G)|-1
 \geq\frac{k}{64e}q^{(s-3)(1-\delta_0/5)}-1.
\]
The choice of $q$ gives
$q\geq(k/s)^{1-\delta_0/2}\geq(k/s)^{1-\delta/2}$ once $L$ is large,
while $k/s\geq L$.  To compare exponents without restricting the original
$\delta$, set
\[
 \beta=(1-\delta_0/2)(1-\delta_0/5).
\]
Since $\delta_0\leq\min\{\delta,1/20\}$,
\[
 1-\beta=\frac7{10}\delta_0-\frac1{10}\delta_0^2
 \leq\frac7{10}\delta.
\]
Consequently
\[
 1+(s-3)\beta-(1-\delta)s
 =\delta s-(s-3)(1-\beta)-2
 \geq\delta s-\frac7{10}\delta(s-3)-2
 =\frac3{10}\delta s+\frac{21}{10}\delta-2>0
\]
once $L=L(\delta)$ is sufficiently large.
More explicitly, the preceding estimates give
\[
 \frac{k}{64e}q^{(s-3)(1-\delta_0/5)}
 \geq \frac{s}{64e}(k/s)^{1+(s-3)\beta}.
\]
If $\delta<1$, the displayed exponent comparison and $k/s\geq L$ imply,
for $L$ sufficiently large, that the right-hand side minus $1$ is at least
$(k/s)^{(1-\delta)s}$.  If $\delta\geq1$, the target power is at most $1$,
whereas $1+(s-3)\beta\geq1$; increasing $L$ ensures that the same right-hand
side is at least $2$, so its difference from $1$ is again at least the target
power.  Hence, after increasing $L$ to meet these fixed-factor inequalities,
substitution into the preceding inequality gives
\[
 r(s,k)\geq(k/s)^{(1-\delta)s}.
\]
All choices of thresholds depend only on $\delta$, as required.
\end{proof}
